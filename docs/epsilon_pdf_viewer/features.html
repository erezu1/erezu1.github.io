Features

Version 1.4.6, November 2026

Search

  • Formula search

    Open the search with ⌘ F and type TeX, e.g. \alpha_1, to find every formula that contains it.

  • Plain queries

    Input without backslashes is read as a formula too, e.g. alpha_1, psi(t,x), n -> \infty, 1/2 m v^2, O(N^-1).

  • Equivalent spellings

    Spellings that draw the same thing match each other, e.g. \sqrt t and \sqrt{t}, \to and \rightarrow, \rm and \mathrm.

  • Words and symbols

    A symbol's name finds the word and the symbol, e.g. phi finds "phi" and φ, log the word and the operator; in $…$, e.g. $phi$, only formulas are searched.

  • Text and formulas together

    A query mixing words and formulas finds the sentence, e.g. "C is a cylinder" finds "C is a cylinder", as does a copied passage with $…$ formulas.

  • Fonts and accents

    A plain letter also finds its other fonts and accented forms, e.g. B finds 𝓑, B̄ and B′, while \mathcal{B} finds only 𝓑.

  • Script positions

    A query without _ or ^ ignores position, e.g. q3 finds q₃, q³ and q3.

  • Hyphenated words

    A word also finds its forms hyphenated at a line end or with a hyphen, e.g. electromagnetic finds electro-magnetic.

  • Citation numbers

    A number in brackets, e.g. [23], finds each citation of that reference, also inside groups ([4, 23, 31]) and ranges ([20–25]), and its entry in the bibliography.

  • Equation numbers

    A number in parentheses, e.g. (2.1), finds that equation, marked on its number.

  • Highlights

    Only the matching symbols are highlighted, not the whole equation.

  • Next and previous match

    ⌘ G and Shift ⌘ G, or Enter and Shift Enter in the search box.

  • Search a selection

    Select text or part of a formula and press ⌘ F to search for it.

Peek

  • Open

    ⌘ + click a link, or right-click it and choose "Open in peek", to show its target in a panel at the bottom while the page above stays where it is.

  • Target mark

    The target is marked: an equation with its number, a figure or table with its caption; a figure is shown whole.

  • Zoom

    The peek opens at the page's zoom; its own − and + buttons, pinch, or ⌘ +/− with the pointer over it zoom the peek alone, and ⌘ 0 returns it to the page's zoom.

  • Resize and expand

    Drag its top edge to resize it; drag it up to the toolbar, or click Expand, to move the main view to the target and close the peek (Back returns).

  • Links in the peek

    A click moves the peek to the link's target; ⌘ + click opens the target in the main view.

  • Close

    Esc, its ✕ button, or Back.

Select and copy

  • Select by formula

    In a formula a selection takes whole elements, e.g. a fraction or bracket entered from outside, and what is highlighted is what is copied; "Select as text" in the ⋮ menu switches to Chrome's own selection.

  • Copy as LaTeX

    ⌘ C copies inline formulas as $…$, displayed equations as \[…\], and part of a formula as the TeX of that part.

  • Copied TeX

    Symbols copy as their commands, e.g. α as \alpha, matrices and cases as environments, and text inside formulas as \text{…}.

  • Copy as Mathematica New in 1.4.6

    Ctrl ⌘ CCtrl Alt C, or "Copy as Mathematica" in the right-click menu, copies the selected formula, or the one under the pointer, as Mathematica input; an ordinary copy where it cannot convert.

  • Keyboard

    With a selection, Shift + ←/→ extends it by one element (a letter with its scripts, a whole fraction), ⌥Ctrl Shift + ←/→ by a word, and Shift + ↑/↓ by a line.

  • Triple click

    Selects the sentence, across lines; on a displayed formula, the whole formula with all its rows, without its number.

  • Submatrix

    Drag from one matrix entry to another to select the block between them, copied as \begin{matrix}…\end{matrix}.

  • Scanned papers

    A scan with a text layer (OCR) can be selected and copied, also when the scan is tilted.

Links

  • Generated links

    In a PDF without internal links, references to sections, equations, figures, tables, theorems, footnotes and citations, and table of contents entries, become links; a PDF with its own links is left as it is.

  • Follow and return

    A click goes to the target; Back returns.

  • Link tooltip

    Hovering a link shows its target and the key to peek it, e.g. "Eq. (2.3) (page 12) · ⌘+click to peek".

Outline and navigation

  • Outline

    The sidebar lists the sections and marks the one in view; a click goes there; its menu also shows the pages as thumbnails.

  • Outline on hover

    Hovering the sidebar button shows the outline over the page; a click docks it beside the page.

  • Generated outline

    A PDF without an outline gets one from its numbered headings (chapters for books), with TeX in titles typeset, e.g. \alpha' as α′.

  • Remembered state

    Each PDF reopens at the last position read, with the sidebar open or closed as left.

  • Narrow windows

    In a narrow window the outline opens over the page and closes once a section is chosen.

  • Navigate in viewer New in 1.4.6

    An option in the ⋮ menu, off by default: jumps from links, the outline and search go into the viewer's own history, with Back and Forward buttons left of the search and ⌘ [/]Alt + ←/→, and Chrome's Back leaves the PDF.

  • First and last page

    Home and End.

Viewer

  • PDFs

    Opens PDFs from the web, and local files once "Allow access to file URLs" is on for the extension in chrome://extensions.

  • Zoom

    The toolbar's − and + or ⌘ +/− zoom about the middle of the view, pinch and Ctrl + scroll about the pointer, and two buttons fit the page or its width.

  • Page layout

    ⋮ menu: "Two pages side by side" or "Single pages", rotate clockwise or counterclockwise, and the hand tool to drag the page.

  • Tab title

    The tab shows the paper's title, read from its first page when the PDF names none.

  • Copy link to page

    ⋮ menu: copies a link that opens the PDF at the current page.

  • Tabs restored

    PDF tabs that Chrome closes when the extension updates reopen at their page.

  • Presentation mode

    ⋮ menu: full screen, one page at a time; Esc leaves it.

  • Print and save

    The toolbar's print and save buttons, or ⌘ P and ⌘ S.

Annotate and sign

  • Highlight

    Select text and click the highlighter in the toolbar; five colours.

  • Text

    The toolbar's text tool adds a text box anywhere on a page.

  • Draw

    The toolbar's draw tool: freehand ink, with colour, thickness and opacity.

  • Sign

    The toolbar's signature tool: type, draw or upload a signature and place it on the page; up to five are kept.

  • Forms

    Fill in a PDF's form fields.

  • Keeping annotations

    Highlights, text, drawings, signatures and filled forms stay only in a saved copy of the PDF (⌘ S); the viewer does not keep them.

Settings

  • Settings page New in 1.4.6

    Click the ε in Chrome's toolbar, or Settings at the end of the ⋮ menu; it also sets the default zoom and the sidebar's state on opening.

  • Look and theme

    Look: Epsilon's or Chrome's; Theme: light, dark, or as the system is set.

  • Link borders

    Off by default; on, it draws the coloured boxes some PDFs put round their links.

  • Signatures

    Turns the signature tool off or on.

  • Sync

    With Chrome Sync on, settings are copied to your other computers.

LaTeX support

  • Publishers

    arXiv preprints and journal PDFs (e.g. APS Physical Review, Springer and JHEP, Elsevier, IOP, Cambridge University Press) are read as they are typeset.

  • Fonts

    Computer Modern, Latin Modern, Times (txfonts, newtx), Palatino (pxfonts, mathpazo), STIX and STIX Two, MathTime, MnSymbol, and publishers' own math fonts.

  • Layout rules

    Fraction bars, roots, overlines, scripts and limits are told apart by TeX's own placement rules for the font in use.

  • Structures

    Fractions, roots, scripts and limits, matrices and cases, sized brackets, accents, \atop and \genfrac, and multi-row displays.

  • Symbols

    AMS symbols, bold symbols (\boldsymbol), and blackboard, calligraphic, script and fraktur letters.

  • Plain TeX

    Papers set in plain TeX or harvmac (e.g. \pmatrix, \eqalign, \cal) are read as their LaTeX equivalents.

  • Figure labels

    Labels made with matplotlib's mathtext are read as formulas.

Privacy

  • Local

    Formulas are read in your browser; PDFs, searches and browsing are sent nowhere, and the extension has no account and no analytics.

  • Stored

    Settings, each PDF's last position and saved signatures stay in Chrome's storage, and go when the extension is removed.
