    The cosmological necklace problem

    Andreas Blommaert, Jonah Kudler-Flam, Vladimir Narovlansky, Erez Y. Urbach

    Euclidean axion wormholes were proposed to give cosmological observers an entropy.

    What it finds
    • Gluing copies of a 3d axion wormhole makes “necklace” saddles of unbounded entropy: the Euclidean sum diverges.
    • In minisuperspace a (mostly) Lorentzian lapse contour lets one necklace dominate, finite in entropy, through a zero-lapse pole from identifying scale factors and .
    • The dominant necklace is partly Lorentzian, not purely Euclidean.
    • Its candidate entropy, , is puzzlingly empty de Sitter's, whatever the axion flux.
    • Axion numerics in 4d and 5d, and Yang–Mills magnetic necklaces, again give one dominant necklace.
  • arXiv:2608.21511hep-thAugust 2026

    String stars, small black holes, and the supersymmetric index

    Erez Y. Urbach

    Do string-scale black holes become self-gravitating “string stars”? The exact supersymmetric index could tell.

    What it finds
    • Near the heterotic index a Higgsed supergravity replaces Atick–Witten; the Hagedorn instability is its winding W boson's Ambjørn–Olesen condensation in a magnetic field.
    • W-boson condensates, “W stars”, resemble Horowitz–Polchinski stars but decouple at the index.
    • Type II's light windings open a T-dual dimension, where a similar string star arguably exists and decouples.
    • Charged string stars seemingly link charged black holes to free strings; at the index both decouple.

  • arXiv:2510.17951hep-thOctober 2025

    A spin on Hagedorn temperatures and string stars

    Josef Seitz, Erez Y. Urbach

    Does a string-size black hole become a self-gravitating “string star” when it rotates?

    What it finds
    • At imaginary angular velocity , a thermal winding mode turns tachyonic, giving exact Hagedorn temperatures for bosonic, type II and heterotic strings.
    • A “rotating string star” saddle, found numerically to order for , rotates rigidly with angular momentum.
    • Extrapolated, its angular momentum and eccentricity match rotating black holes' and free strings' up to factors: a qualitative interpolation.
    • In AdS, the Hagedorn temperature's leading shift is computed at real or imaginary rotation.
  • arXiv:2505.14771hep-thMay 2025

    Absolute entropy and the observer’s no-boundary state

    Andreas Blommaert, Jonah Kudler-Flam, Erez Y. Urbach

    In closed universes, observer algebras fix entropy up to an constant.

    What it finds
    • The observer's no-boundary density matrix is argued to be the identity (maximum entropy): its path integral computes inner products.
    • Geometrically, the state is a bra-ket wormhole; the worldline forbids the Hartle–Hawking cap.
    • In Jackiw–Teitelboim gravity, wormhole amplitudes match the identity.
    • No-boundary expectation values are a trace for the observer's algebra, defining entropy relative to that state.
    • With horizons, the state is again the identity on the observer's region, consistent with all previously discussed traces for invariantly defined regions.

    The black hole/string transition in AdS and confining backgrounds

    Erez Y. Urbach

    String stars, thermal winding condensates, may describe string-scale black holes; the paper studies AdS and confining backgrounds.

    What it finds
    • AdS curvature binds string stars, unlike flat ; they grow to AdS size as NS-NS flux replaces RR. A possible BTZ/string transition could link them to a winding condensate on the BTZ.

  • arXiv:2207.11980hep-thJuly 2022

    Onset of Quantum Chaos in Random Field Theories

    Micha Berkooz, Adar Sharon, Navot Silberstein, Erez Y. Urbach

    SYK-like disorder gives the chaos exponent , usually only at infinite coupling. How does chaos start?

    What it finds
    • Randomly coupled copies of any CFT obey SYK-like large- equations; their ladder kernel gives a putative exponent.
    • Exactly marginal disorder keeps the theory conformal at every coupling, so can be followed from weak to strong.
    • Disordered generalized free fields have weak-coupling putative exponent : a discontinuous onset, at finite coupling.
    • A disordered supersymmetric minimal model's putative exponent tends to zero at small coupling: a continuous onset is expected.
  • arXiv:2202.06966hep-thFebruary 2022

    String Stars in Anti de Sitter Space

    Erez Y. Urbach

    A black hole naively extrapolated to the string scale roughly agrees with a “string star” of hot strings; here, in AdS.

    What it finds
    • Somewhat below the Hagedorn temperature, the AdS star is the flat-space Horowitz–Polchinski solution.
    • Hotter, its size stays bounded; it fades into the thermal gas.
    • That is the AdS Hagedorn temperature, hotter than flat space's by a fraction .
    • Near it a negative mode is argued for; farther away, AdS-sized extra dimensions should make such modes Gregory–Laflamme-like.

    The onset of quantum chaos in disordered CFTs

    Micha Berkooz, Adar Sharon, Navot Silberstein, Erez Y. Urbach

    The chaos exponent is known mostly at isolated points; how chaos grows with coupling is open.

    What it finds
    • Disorder is exactly marginal among generalized free fields (in cSYK at leading order in ) and, for every realization, among supersymmetric minimal models.
    • For generalized free fields the kernel allows no positive exponent below a finite , so chaos is absent: a discontinuous transition.
    • For the simplest supersymmetric minimal model, chaos should grow from zero coupling.
    • Large- self-consistency equations give any disordered CFT's two- and four-point functions; OTOCs keep a ladder structure.
  • arXiv:2109.05512hep-thSeptember 2021

    AdS from CFT for scalar QED

    Ofer Aharony, Shai M. Chester, Erez Y. Urbach

    The authors had rewritten free scalars' singlet sector as gravity in AdS, deriving AdS/CFT there. Can a gauge field be added?

    What it finds
    • For scalar QED in the photon becomes a bulk vector's boundary value; the explicit low-energy dual, non-local with couplings, is the free one with new boundary conditions.
    • In three dimensions a Chern–Simons term makes the boundary condition mixed; then no dressed monopole is gauge invariant, so monopole operators are excluded.
    • Scalar QCD with fixed colour number gets a very similar, adjoint-valued dual.
  • arXiv:2105.15059hep-thMay 2021

    The entanglement entropy of typical pure states and replica wormholes

    Erez Y. Urbach

    A typical pure state on a circle should show a Page curve in an interval's entanglement entropy, but lacks a thermal state's simple path integral.

    What it finds
    • Averaging the Rényi entropy over an ensemble of pure states writes it as a path integral on replicas identified at : a singular geometry.
    • At high temperature the bulk's thermal saddle dominates short intervals and a replica-joining saddle, allowed by that geometry, past half the circle: a Page curve, absent at low temperature.
    • The RT surface may then be homologous to the interval or its complement.

    A Derivation of AdS/CFT for Vector Models

    Ofer Aharony, Shai M. Chester, Erez Y. Urbach

    What bulk does a CFT define beyond ? Simplest case: vector models, dual to high-spin gravity.

    What it finds
    • Matching conformal eigenmodes of with AdS modes gives a path integral over all-spin AdS fields.
    • The non-local bulk action is presumably Vasiliev's classical theory, gauge-fixed; its quadratic part can be made local, with Vasiliev's spectrum.
    • Loops are computable: counter-terms are argued to cancel all divergences.
    • In principle finite works: products of bulk fields obey constraints excluding even empty AdS.
    • The critical model keeps the action; only the scalar's boundary condition changes.

2018

  • arXiv:1806.01237cond-mat.softJune 2018

    The metric description of viscoelasticity and instabilities in viscoelastic solids

    Erez Y. Urbach, Efi Efrati

    Rubbery solids can creep, then snap, though relaxation should only stabilize them.

    What it finds
    • Viscoelasticity becomes elasticity about a reference metric drifting from rest towards recent shapes.
    • In incompressible isotropic solids, a stable shape reached suddenly from rest never moves or destabilizes.
    • In incompressible isotropic solids only acquired stability can be lost, and it always is unless the shape is a rest equilibrium.
    • A spring–dashpot truss illustrates the rules: bistability absent, lasting, or acquired then lost.
    • Rubber “jumping poppers” that snap back late had acquired their inverted stability.

2017

  • arXiv:1711.09491cond-mat.softNovember 2017

    Delayed instabilities in viscoelastic solids through a metric description

    Erez Y. Urbach, Efi Efrati

    Viscoelastic structures, like elastomer shells, can creep, then snap; standard models give little intuition about which will.

    What it finds
    • Viscoelasticity is recast exactly as elasticity about a “reference metric”, local rest lengths that creep towards the present shape.
    • A body brought abruptly from rest into a stable shape never moves, though its stress relaxes.
    • So late snaps need shapes unstable at rest that creep stabilised while held; that stability is eventually lost if not initially extremal.
    • For about 50 rubber poppers, predicted stability phases match experiment, given one measured constant.
